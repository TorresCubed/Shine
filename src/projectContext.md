Shine — Project Context

Concept: Sokoban-style haunted-mansion puzzle game (working title "Shine," not final). Small character carries a light source, explores in fog-of-war darkness. Grid-based movement/obstacles, locked doors, laser mechanics gated behind lever-controlled hidden mirrors (laser sits inert until mirrors are aligned via a separate puzzle).

Core design pillar: Light must be 100% physically real — actual reflection/refraction simulation, not simplified 90-degree grid bounces. This is the central engineering showcase of the project, not a stretch goal.

Stack/platform choices:

Web-based: Canvas/WebGL, TypeScript/React, wrapped with Capacitor for mobile deployment
Chosen over Unity/Godot specifically because it reinforces the existing TS/React stack and is a stronger portfolio/engineering signal (manual raycasting math vs. an engine's built-in lighting)
First platform target: mobile
Being built as a portfolio piece while job searching — not a competitive/coworker project, not a pivot into game dev

Mechanics so far:

Character moves freely (any direction, PLAYER_SPEED), but collision stays on the grid: a square footprint can't overlap a wall cell (and slides along walls), or cross a door that isn't fully open. Interactions (plates, pickups, drop spot, the exit) use the cell the player's centre is in; pushing a box triggers snap-to-grid
Single point light source at launch (no color/prism/polarization — explicitly future work)
Flashlight/beam mechanic (replacing laser for most puzzles) — shines forward for extended sight range; separate from close-range candle/lantern glow
Hold at most one light at a time; lights can be dropped (they keep shining) and picked back up, so the candle and flashlight can both be out if one is on the floor. Fear rule: light is required for movement (see levels.md)
Placed-light use case: aim flashlight at a mirror, spin the mirror, watch for reflection hitting a target as angle feedback
Levers/buttons have hidden, undiscoverable-until-tried effects; mirror angles have no direct correctness feedback until tested

Current implementation state (canvas prototype), by file:

consts.ts: tuning values only. state.ts: all mutable game state (player, lights, doors, mirrors, level geometry) and loadLevel.
levels.ts: levels as text maps (legend at the top). Each level([...], options) call adds the next numbered level; options set the starting lights/aim, doors and mirrors. Triggers are digits on the map; doors aren't on the map but in the `doors` option, keyed by trigger number, on edges between cells ({ between: '3,4 3,5', opens: 'up' }, optional kind/hinge); a trigger is a light plate unless its door kind is lever. Mirrors are '-', '|', '/', '\'.
rayTracer.ts: forward ray tracing (castLight). Rays bounce off mirrors until they hit a wall or run out of range/bounces; neighbouring rays form strips grouped by mirror chain, each with one virtual source the falloff is centred on (so falloff tracks true path length). brightnessAt measures light with the same LIGHT_FALLOFF_STOPS the renderer draws with.
scene.ts: walls and mirrors as a flat segment list for the tracer, rebuilt only when geometry changes.
playerLogic.ts: free movement with grid collision (walls, doors not fully open) plus a small pivot post (MIRROR_PIVOT_RADIUS) for mirrors (the rest of a mirror passes over you), corner assist, sliding (stopped dead by the dark, a pivot or a slanted door, you slide off at up to SLIDE_MAX_DEG, moving the cos of the turn), and the fear rule (empty-handed you can only move to points lit to LIT_THRESHOLD, with FEAR_REACH leniency; a dropped light's cell always counts as lit). Space is the one action key, doing the first of: pull a lever in your cell (toggling its door and turning the mirrors linked to it); turn a turnable mirror in your cell; pick up the light on your cell; turn a turnable mirror you're facing within MIRROR_REACH; drop your light (never on a plate, mirror or lever). A dropped light lands exactly where you stand (FloorLight x, y), not snapped to the cell's centre, and is picked up from within PICKUP_RADIUS of where it lies (no dropping within that of another), but still belongs to its cell for the fear rule's lit cell; a dropped flashlight's handle-end source is pulled in so it never starts behind a wall, door or mirror. Touch (touchControls.ts): a press that drags becomes a floating stick centred where it went down (state.stick; barely pushed = turn/aim in place, further = walk at a speed that grows with the push, snapping to straight/diagonal within STICK_SNAP_DEG); after the first touch, on-screen swap/restart buttons show and the win card says "Tap to continue". Taps/clicks (a press that stays within TAP_SLOP → renderer screenToWorld → tapAt, or next level on the win card): on the player (TAP_PLAYER_RADIUS) picks up or drops; on a lever or turnable mirror within TAP_REACH with a clear line, pulls or turns it. A mirror turns a step (swinging over you if you stand beside it); mirrors swing round smoothly (MIRROR_STEP_MS per step) and light, collision and drawing follow the swing. Dropping a light takes out the pocketed one. Lights: carry at most one of each kind, one lit in hand; F swaps, the flashlight faces the way you walk (swinging round at FACING_TURN_DEG_PER_S, and you only set off once facing within WALK_FACING_TOLERANCE_DEG); standing still, Q/E aim it (slow start, ramps up). Mirrors are fixed, turnable, or lever-turned (level option); nothing shows which yet.
doorLogic.ts: light doors follow their plate, lever doors their lever; locked doors unlock while their plate is lit and open for good when walked into. A door is one or more leaves, each on an edge between two cells, swinging 90° about its hinge into its `into` cell (state.doorLeaves gives the leaf segments at an open amount, eased). Leaves are absorbing segments in the scene (so light spills through mid-swing), block movement as thin segments (plus the whole closed edge until fully open), and a door holds still while the player is in a cell it swings through. Items on the floor don't block doors.
renderer.ts: the lights are the one in hand, dropped ones, and wall lamps (the start square has no glow of its own). All light is added together ('lighter'), then cut to the player's line of sight (computeView, which bounces off mirrors, so you see what a visible mirror reflects). Floor-level things (markers, lamps, plates, levers, pickups, mirrors) are painted into both the lit floor and the remembered floor, so they're hidden in darkness and remembered in fog. Walls show only where light reaches their faces, shaded by incidence and beam profile but never brighter than the wall art; where the flashlight's centre hits a wall, the wall art fades in as a spot. The held light shines from where it is in the player sprite (the flame, or filling the flashlight's lens), pulled back so it never starts past a wall. Fog memory: a low-res grayscale mask of the brightest each spot has been seen lit ('lighten' = per-pixel max). Rule: fog never updates what can't be seen, so doors and mirrors are drawn in fog as last seen (except a mirror turn you cause, which you see complete, and a locked door you've seen before flashing green/red for LOCK_FLASH_MS when it unlocks/re-locks, a stand-in until it has art). Camera follows the player over a level-sized world canvas; each new level starts zoomed exactly to fit (main.ts fitZoom; the one non-whole zoom above 1x, kept fitted on resize/rotate until you zoom), then zoom with wheel or +/-, or pinch (continuous mid-pinch, settling on a whole step or back to fit within PINCH_FIT_SNAP). Two fingers also drag the view (camera.pan, eased back as you walk). Max zoom is CAMERA_MAX_ZOOM art px per CSS px. The screen canvas has one pixel per physical pixel; zoom is whole steps from 1x to CAMERA_MAX_ZOOM with smoothing off and whole-pixel camera offsets, so pixel art stays crisp, and continuous (smoothed) below 1x only to fit a big level. Performance: the camera is worked out before drawing, and the fog layer and lit regions are only composited within the on-screen part of the world (visibleRect), with each light's blends clipped to its bounds; lights are drawn as one shared light map (each light adds its polygons at half strength and its wall/door faces, as quads into the solid, at full, faded by its falloff, into litLayer's alpha), then the lit art (litArt: floor, marks, tint, wall and door colours, rebuilt only when something on it changes) is laid in once with 'source-in'. A canvas can't store more than 100%, so anything brighter than the art (strong overlapping lights, wall glow, the flashlight hotspot's glow) is capped for now (a second, additive pass would restore it); the recent memory is kept inverted so it fades on the GPU. ?perf shows a per-phase CPU timing breakdown; GPU cost is found by switching parts off with ?skip=fog,memory,lit,floor,faces,sight,objects,screen and comparing fps (reading pixels back to time the GPU makes Chrome move canvases off the GPU).
Game feel (all drawing only; gameplay light stays steady): flames flicker (FLAME_FLICKER_*, a seed per flame shared by its reflections); dust motes drift everywhere but are drawn into each light's region before it's cut and faded, so they only show in light (DUST_*); mirror turns have momentum (accelerate, then slow to stop exactly, so repeated presses extend a turn without stopping); doors and zoom ease (util.easeInOut); a scale punch on pickup/drop/swap/lever (state.punch, PUNCH_*); an idle breathe/bob exists but is off (IDLE_STYLE) pending hand-drawn frames. Screen shake/flash (incl. feedback when the fear rule stops you) is planned. Level transitions (transition.ts: eased Fades for the screen's darkness and the Level Complete card): reaching the exit dims the world and fades the card in (with room left for a candle flame under the title); Enter fades to black and the next level fades in as its lights warm up; R dips to black and back (main.ts startLevel/leaveTo, renderer drawTransition).
Art (src/assets, hand-drawn; no AI art): 64px per cell, drawn 1:1 with smoothing off. floorboards.png is a one-cell tile. walls.png is autotiled: baked once per level so the rim shows only on sides facing floor (outer corners, running edges and inner corners built from the one tile; WALL_RIM is its depth). Fog memory uses grayed, darkened copies of both. Object art (OBJECT_SPRITES in consts.ts: mirror, dropped/unfound flashlight, dropped candle) is drawn turned about its anchor, with a fog copy; the mirror art is upright ('|') with its face on the anchor column, the flashlight points right with the anchor on its lens line. Player sprites (PLAYER_SPRITES in consts.ts) face right, one per light in hand plus empty-handed, each with the head anchor and light position in image pixels.
Level editor (editor.html, src/editor/): paint a map, set mirror angles and control, door kinds and pairs, and starting lights, and export the level([...], {...}) code for levels.ts. levelCode.ts converts between the editor's grid and the text maps/options (levels.ts keeps each level's source in levelSources for this); preview.ts traces a level's light with the real ray tracer (lamps, candles, plus "try" lights that aren't part of the level; light and locked doors open while their plate is lit, re-traced until stable); solver.ts finds every way to win a flashlight-only level (mirror settings x drop cell x aim, then a fear-rule walk out); editor.ts is the page (plain/fog/bright views, 15% line, plate brightness, solution preview, playtest). Playtest opens the game at /?playtest&view=fog|bright with the level from localStorage ('shine-playtest'); the renderer's view modes there show the whole level (V cycles fog/bright/normal). Every edit is built with the game's own fromMap, so mistakes show in the editor. Work is kept in localStorage.
main.ts: canvases, asset loading, input, level progression (starts on the newest level while designing, or level N with ?level=N; Enter = next level, R = restart).
Levels: 1-3 darkness, 4-6 plates/doors/dropping the candle/locked door, 7 lamps and light adding up, 8-11 the flashlight, 12-15 two lights at once (14 is the chain capstone), 16-23 mirrors (flashlight only: chains, diagonals, two turnable, a two-stage locked door, two doors in a row, order of operations, capstone), 24-26 levers (a lever door, a lever-turned mirror, one lever working a door and a mirror). Level puzzles are checked with a scratch solver that runs the real ray tracer over every mirror setting, drop cell and aim, walking out under the fear rule.
